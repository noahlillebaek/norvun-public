Introduction

Norvun is building an integrated intelligence ecosystem across financial statements, economic relationships, capital flows and investment research. Its Financial Intelligence, Economic Intelligence and Capital Flow Intelligence products address complementary aspects of the financial world.

This research note examines a single selected application of statistical inference within Economic Intelligence: the probabilistic estimation of U.S. business-cycle states.

02

The research question

How can an economy's unobserved state be inferred from incomplete, temporally dependent evidence?

Norvun's U.S. business-cycle research examines this question through competing statistical approaches, historical release vintages and retrospective evaluation. The research separates state estimation, transition uncertainty and the assessment of predictive probabilities.

03

Mathematical methodology

The research extends fundamental principles of probability, stochastic variables and statistical estimation to four related mathematical questions.

3.1Conditional probability

Latent-state inference

The first task is to estimate the current state from the available information.

P(St=s|It)
(1)

Given information available at time t, estimate the probability of an unobserved economic state Sₜ. The state is represented as a discrete stochastic variable; its conditional probability distribution assigns a probability to each possible state.

3.2Conditional probability distributions

Regime transitions

Once the current state has been estimated, the question shifts to its possible evolution.

P(St+h=j|It)
(2)

The future economic state is a stochastic variable. Its conditional distribution describes the probabilities of possible states at a given horizon. An endpoint state probability is not the same as the probability of leaving the current regime at least once within the horizon.

3.3Probability distributions

Survival and duration analysis

The evolution of an economic state also raises a question about its remaining duration.

P(T>h|It)=∏k=1h(1−λk)
(3)

For a discrete residual lifetime T, λₖ is the conditional probability of exit in period k, given survival to that period and the information set. This survival probability describes the distribution of remaining duration, from which an expected duration can be derived when finite. Duration estimates depend on assumptions about the conditional exit probabilities (hazards).

3.4Squared prediction errors

Probability evaluation

These estimates must ultimately be evaluated against observed outcomes.

BS=1n∑i=1n(pi−yi)2
(4)

For a binary outcome yᵢ, the Brier score evaluates probability predictions pᵢ by squared loss. As a mean squared prediction error for a binary outcome, it evaluates probabilistic predictions rather than the variance of a parameter estimator. Interpretation requires comparison with appropriate baselines, calibration and discrimination metrics, and the intended economic use.

04

Statistical evaluation and model selection

4.1 Model complexity and estimation risk

Does additional model complexity improve probability estimation, or does it introduce unnecessary estimation risk? The bias–variance trade-off provides a statistical lens for this question: greater flexibility may reduce some forms of systematic error but increase estimation variability. The research compares statistical approaches rather than presuming a more complex model is better. Probability scores and turning-point detection address different objectives.

4.2 Historical validation and temporal dependence

Validation matters as much as estimation. Dated information sets, release-aware reference labels and retrospective out-of-sample evaluation address look-ahead risk. Serial dependence means successive observations cannot generally be treated as independent and identically distributed (iid). Overlapping horizons, limited independent recession episodes and model-selection effects constrain interpretation. Software tests and statistical performance are separate forms of evidence.

4.3 Scope and limitations

This research overview presents selected mathematical frameworks and validation principles. Detailed evaluation results, model specifications and current regime estimates remain proprietary.
